# High-Impact Journal Writing Guardrails

Use this reference when polishing existing English manuscript text for a Nature/Science/Cell-leaning style. These rules are transferable writing principles, not a substitute for target-journal author instructions.

Sources:

- Nature formatting guide: https://www.nature.com/nature/for-authors/formatting-guide
- Nature initial submission guide: https://www.nature.com/documents/nature_3a_initial_revised_submissions.pdf
- Science Partner Journals author guidelines: https://spj.science.org/page/research/for-authors
- Cell Reports information for authors: https://apps.lib.whu.edu.cn/ensci/editnew/upfile/information_for_authors.pdf
- Cell Press graphical abstract guidelines: https://crosstalk.cell.com/hubfs/Files/GA_guide.pdf
- Broad Institute abstract guide: https://mitcommlab.mit.edu/broad/commkit/journal-article-abstract/

## Global stance

- Prioritize clarity, evidence boundaries, reproducibility and reader navigation over decorative prose.
- Keep terminology stable. Do not vary technical nouns for style.
- Each section has a job; do not move Results into the Introduction or turn Discussion into a repeated Results section.
- Preserve caveats, sample scope, data type and statistical qualifiers.

## Title

- Keep the title concise, specific and understandable to a broad scientific audience.
- Avoid unnecessary abbreviations, overly technical terms and active verbs when they overstate mechanism.
- Do not use causal or mechanistic title language unless directly supported.

## Abstract

An effective abstract usually follows this order:

1. Broad context.
2. Specific knowledge gap.
3. What this study did.
4. Main result or answer.
5. Implication, bounded by evidence.

Polishing rules:

- Do not turn the abstract into a full data inventory.
- Avoid citations, undefined abbreviations and excessive method detail unless required.
- Keep only the numbers needed to support the central conclusion.
- End with implication, not speculation.

## Introduction

- Build from field context to specific gap to study objective.
- Provide only the background needed to understand the question.
- Do not preload detailed Results.
- The final paragraph should make the study question or rationale explicit.

## Results

- Results should state observations under defined conditions.
- Use subheadings to guide the reader when the manuscript has multiple result modules.
- Each paragraph should have one controlling claim.
- Mention only key values needed to support the paragraph claim.
- Avoid mechanistic verbs unless the evidence directly tests mechanism.

## Result subsection structure

- Opening paragraph: orient the reader to the question, dataset, analysis object and expected conclusion direction.
- Evidence paragraphs: develop one claim at a time, with the minimum necessary quantitative support.
- Closing paragraph: summarize what this subsection has established and provide a transition.

## Discussion

- Explain significance, scope, limitations, alternatives and future directions.
- Do not recapitulate the Results.
- Separate supported conclusions from hypotheses.
- Some journal families discourage figure/table citations in Discussion; follow the target journal when known.

## Methods

- Preserve reproducibility details: data source, version, software, parameters, filtering, statistics and sample definition.
- Do not polish away necessary technical detail.
- Methods should be concise but sufficient for interpretation and replication.

## Figure legends

- A figure legend should be understandable with minimal dependence on the main text.
- Begin with a short title-like sentence stating what the figure depicts.
- Define panels, sample sizes, symbols, colors, statistics and abbreviations.
- Avoid Discussion-style interpretation unless the journal specifically expects a conclusion-oriented legend.
