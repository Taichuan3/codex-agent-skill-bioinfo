# High-Impact Journal Translation Guidance

Use this reference when translating Chinese manuscript text into English while preserving section function and evidence boundaries. These are transferable principles from Nature/Science/Cell-facing author guidance, not a target-journal format checklist.

Sources:

- Nature formatting guide: https://www.nature.com/nature/for-authors/formatting-guide
- Nature initial submission guide: https://www.nature.com/documents/nature_3a_initial_revised_submissions.pdf
- Science Partner Journals author guidelines: https://spj.science.org/page/research/for-authors
- Cell Reports information for authors: https://apps.lib.whu.edu.cn/ensci/editnew/upfile/information_for_authors.pdf
- Cell Press graphical abstract guidelines: https://crosstalk.cell.com/hubfs/Files/GA_guide.pdf
- Broad Institute abstract guide: https://mitcommlab.mit.edu/broad/commkit/journal-article-abstract/
- Development manuscript preparation: https://journals.biologists.com/dev/pages/manuscript-prep

## Translation stance

- Translate the section's function, not only sentence meaning.
- Do not import Chinese rhetorical repetition if it weakens English structure.
- Keep one Chinese concept mapped to one English term.
- Preserve evidence boundaries before making the English more polished.

## Abstract translation

Preferred order:

1. Context.
2. Knowledge gap.
3. Study action or approach.
4. Main result.
5. Bounded implication.

Avoid:

- Translating every result detail into the abstract.
- Adding broad claims not present in the Chinese source.
- Using undefined abbreviations or citation-like phrases.

## Introduction translation

- Translate background as a logical narrowing sequence: field -> known problem -> unresolved gap -> current study.
- Avoid making the introduction sound as if the results are already proven before the study is introduced.
- The final sentence or paragraph should clarify the question, rationale or objective.

## Results translation

- Results English should be concrete and evidence-facing.
- Keep the controlling claim at the start of a paragraph when possible.
- Use observed, identified, detected, found, was associated with, was consistent with according to evidence strength.
- Avoid proved, revealed the mechanism, drove, determined unless directly supported.

## Discussion translation

- Discussion English can interpret, but must signal limits.
- Use may, suggest, support, are consistent with, raise the possibility that when evidence is indirect.
- Do not repeat Results sentence-by-sentence.
- Separate conclusions from model, hypothesis or future work.

## Methods translation

- Preserve reproducibility language even if it sounds technical.
- Do not compress away versions, parameters, filtering rules, sample definitions or statistical tests.
- Prefer precise verbs: aligned, filtered, normalized, annotated, modeled, tested.

## Figure legend translation

- A legend should state what is depicted, define panels and explain symbols, colors, sample sizes, statistics and abbreviations.
- Do not turn a figure legend into a discussion paragraph.
- Keep panel order and terminology consistent with the figure and main text.

## Output addition

When using this reference, add a short `Section-function note` if the Chinese source needs restructuring before translation.
