# Clinical Evidence and Safety Boundary

## Evidence classes

| Evidence | Preserve | Can support | Cannot establish alone |
|---|---|---|---|
| Trial registry | ID, status/date, phase, arms, eligibility, prespecified outcomes | study existence and registered design | efficacy or safety |
| Trial result | population, analysis set, endpoint/timepoint, effect and uncertainty, harms | endpoint-specific study result | generalized benefit outside the study |
| Clinical assertion | condition, allele/entity, review level, date, conflicts | curated clinical context | diagnosis for an individual |
| PGx evidence | gene/allele definition, drug, phenotype, population, evidence level, date | research drug-gene context | individual dose or prescription |
| Label/guideline | jurisdiction/body, section, version/date, population | current authoritative context | individualized application without clinical assessment |
| Safety signal | report source, event definition, exposure context, denominator availability | signal requiring evaluation | incidence or causality without valid design |
| Biomarker/model | intended use, cohort, endpoint, validation, calibration | bounded association or prediction performance | clinical utility or personal prognosis |

## PGx and regulatory review

1. Normalize gene, allele/haplotype, phenotype and drug terminology.
2. Record population, evidence level, source version/date and whether the statement is informative, recommended or required within that source.
3. Keep label, guideline, association study and functional evidence as separate rows.
4. Report discordance and missing applicability data.
5. Stop at research context; do not translate the table into dose, drug choice or medication change for a person.

## Safety-signal review

- Distinguish spontaneous report, disproportionality signal, observational rate and randomized comparison.
- Record duplicate handling, exposure denominator, confounding, stimulated reporting and time window.
- Use "signal" when causality or incidence is unsupported.

## Personal-request response

When a request asks what a person should diagnose, take, stop, change, screen or expect:

1. State that the evidence review cannot provide individualized medical advice.
2. Do not calculate or imply a personal clinical action from research data.
3. Offer a bounded explanation of the general evidence and what fields a qualified clinician would need.
4. Encourage review by an appropriate licensed professional without selecting a treatment.
5. Avoid exposing patient-identifiable details in any external query or artifact.

This safety boundary does not weaken legitimate research synthesis; it prevents a change in intended use.
